# PRD Quality Review — prd-xrpl-wallet-2026-09-12

## Overall verdict

As a record of v1 as built, this PRD holds up structurally better than most: every one of the fifteen `docs/` pointers resolves, FR-1 through FR-56 are contiguous and unique with no duplicates, the §5/§6.2 boundary double-files nothing, and the two drift claims it makes about the source record (the Address Book's absence from `docs/user-stories/INDEX.md`, and `docs/decisions.md` §2's "**Yes, add as a new epic**") are both accurate against the files. What is at risk is not the scaffolding but three places where the PRD's own words do not agree with each other or with the record it defers to: §3's glossary leaves **Seed** undefined while defining **Master Key** as something XRPL already uses that term for — the documentary form of the exact defect `addendum.md` §2 records as its most load-bearing reasoning; §5 asserts *permanent* deferral where `decisions.md` says "for now" and "in v1", and §2.2/§6.2 split on whether multi-signing is a permanent boundary or an MVP deferral; and FR-52's flat "never cached" contradicts NFR-3 and FR-12, which both presuppose a query cache of ledger data. §10's open questions are, with two exceptions, not closable as written.

## 1. Decision-readiness — adequate

Decisions are stated as decisions throughout §4 and §8, with the give-up named rather than smoothed: SM-C1 ("Do not optimize for fewer confirmation steps. … Friction before an irreversible action is the product working") and SM-C3 ("adding gates is not the same as verifying") are counter-metrics that cost something, not decoration. §9.1's "where speed and verifiability conflict, verifiability wins" is a real tie-breaker a reader can apply. §6.2's RTL entry — "recorded as genuinely unreviewed rather than dismissed" — and §9.6's evidence-discipline paragraph are the opposite of smoothing.

The two `[NOTE FOR PM]` callouts sit at real tensions, not safe checkpoints. But they are resolved unequally, and the *less* tracked one is the one the PRD itself calls more dangerous. §9.3's CDN callout is routed: it names CD-6, points at `docs/sprints/cicd-sprints.md`, and `addendum.md` §4 carries CD-6 as a live residual risk with two siblings. §6.2's RPC-endpoint callout asserts "This is the deferral most likely to be missed by an operator who does not trust the shipped endpoints" — and then stops. No §10 question covers it, and `addendum.md` §3 lists "User-editable RPC endpoints | Deferred to post-v1" with no risk framing at all. The PRD identifies its highest-miss-risk deferral and then tracks it nowhere.

§10 is where decision-readiness thins. Q6 ("Are the stale open-decision tables a correctness risk?") answers itself in its own next sentence — "Reported, not repaired — outside this PRD's task" — and `addendum.md` §7 already adjudicates it as documentation drift. That is the rubric's rhetorical-question pattern. Q1, Q4 and Q5 carry no closure condition: Q4 states outright "no owner and no revisit condition set", and Q5 asks "What triggers the first real Mainnet funding?" about the precondition of SM-3, the metric §7 calls "the only adoption metric that exists" — leaving the PRD's headline success criterion gated on an undefined trigger. Only Q2 (test on an Android device) and Q3 (ask whether bunny.net exposes a scoped credential) are closable by an identifiable act.

### Findings
- **medium** Unrouted `[NOTE FOR PM]` at §6.2 (§6.2, user-editable RPC endpoints; cf. §9.3, `addendum.md` §3/§4) — the callout the PRD names as "the deferral most likely to be missed" has no §10 question, no residual-risk entry, and no revisit condition, while the §9.3 callout it sits beside is fully routed through CD-6. *Fix:* add a §10 question ("Under what condition does the operator get editable endpoints — distrust of a shipped endpoint, or a failover both endpoints fail?") or an `addendum.md` §4 risk entry, so the two callouts are tracked symmetrically.
- **medium** §10 Q6 is not an open question (§10.6) — it is asked and dismissed in the same entry, and already adjudicated in `addendum.md` §7. *Fix:* delete it from §10, or restate it as the one genuinely open part — whether a future reader can be misled by V1–V4/N1–N4 tables that still say "open", and who repairs them.
- **medium** Three open questions have no closure condition (§10.1, §10.4, §10.5) — Q5 in particular leaves SM-3's precondition undefined while §7 calls SM-3 the product's only adoption metric. *Fix:* give each a decision trigger rather than an owner (the operator is the only owner): Q1 "decide when a 320px device is next in hand", Q5 "the manual Testnet pass plus a byte-identical rebuild verified against the published tag".

## 2. Substance over theater — strong

Nothing here reads as furniture. §1's Vision could not swap into another wallet PRD: "A credential that cannot decrypt the vault cannot unlock the app" and "A wallet that is pleasant but wrong about money has failed completely" are this product's specific commitments. §8's NFRs are the antithesis of NFR boilerplate — NFR-1 names the banned call (`Number()` on a Drops value), NFR-5 names the banned declaration (`outline-none`), NFR-6 names the gate binary (`bun run check:contrast`), NFR-9 names the numbers (320px, 200%). There is no differentiation section written because a template had one, and §2.3 refuses persona theater on the record rather than supplying it. The one structural absence — no `[NON-GOAL for MVP]` inline callouts — is defensible because §5 and §6.2 do the work centrally.

## 3. Strategic coherence — adequate

There is a thesis, stated in §1 and restated as a design rule in §9.1: a wallet that can be *checked* rather than believed. The feature set follows it rather than listing capabilities — §4.10 (explorer cross-check) and §4.12 (user-controlled updates) exist only because of that thesis, and §4.12's description says so explicitly: "This feature is what keeps the no-backend claim honest." Counter-metrics exist and bite (SM-C2 explicitly forbids the caching optimisation that would make SM-1's cross-check feel cheaper). The scope kind — a capability spec for a single operator — matches the shape.

Where coherence frays is the SM→FR mapping, which over-claims in one direction and leaves a hole in the other. SM-1 is defined as "Zero instances of a displayed balance, fee, or amount that disagrees with the ledger" and then claims to validate "FR-16 through FR-30" — a range that sweeps in FR-19 (destination tag entry), FR-21 (first-time-destination warning), FR-25 (frozen line blocks sending), and FR-26 through FR-28 (share address, share tag, incoming notice), none of which is a money-figure claim an explorer cross-check can falsify. Meanwhile roughly half the FR set has no SM pointing at it at all: FR-1–FR-5, FR-10–FR-15, FR-31–FR-43, FR-45, and every versioning FR except FR-50. Some of that is unavoidable, but FR-42's "A link never points at the wrong Network's explorer" is exactly as cross-checkable as SM-1's claims and is unmeasured.

### Findings
- **medium** SM-1's FR range over-claims and the SM set under-covers (§7 SM-1, SM-2; §4.5, §4.7–§4.10) — "FR-16 through FR-30" includes six FRs with no money figure to disagree, while FR-31–FR-43 (trust lines, history, network switching, explorer links) have no success metric despite FR-42's wrong-network claim being directly verifiable. *Fix:* narrow SM-1 to FR-16, FR-18, FR-22–FR-24, FR-29, FR-30; add an SM for the cross-check surface (FR-42/FR-44) and one for NFR-3's wallet/network isolation (FR-14, FR-38), both of which the manual Testnet pass can already falsify.

## 4. Done-ness clarity — adequate

Most FRs carry a testable consequence, and the good ones are very good: FR-8's "fails on the AES-GCM authentication tag, not on an application-level comparison" is falsifiable by reading the code; FR-9's "third consecutive failed attempt" and "eight consecutive failures" are numbers; FR-11 distinguishes coarse-pointer-immediate from desktop-30-second-grace; FR-24's "the same signed blob is never blind-resubmitted" names the prohibited behaviour; FR-35's "the list is complete regardless of Trust Line count" is a property, not an adjective. The §8 NFRs give bounds rather than adjectives throughout. There are no instances of "handles X gracefully" or "reasonable performance" in the document.

Three FRs are not implementable as written, and one of the three is a contradiction rather than a vagueness. FR-52 states "Ledger data is network-first and never cached" — but FR-12 requires locking to clear "cached balances, history, and the query cache", and NFR-3 mandates that "Every ledger read goes through a query hook" whose key includes the Active Wallet and the Active Network. A query hook with a cache key *is* a cache of ledger data. NFR-4 says the precise thing FR-52 means — "The service worker caches the static shell only. Never an RPC response" — so the contradiction is FR-52's unqualified wording, and it matters because SM-C2 elevates it to a policy ("Caching an RPC response to make a balance appear faster is the failure mode this product is built to avoid"), which as written would forbid the query layer NFR-3 requires.

FR-21 and FR-45 are mutually circular without ever defining membership. FR-21 fires when sending to "an address not in the Address Book"; FR-45 says "The Address Book is what FR-21 tests 'first time' against." Neither says whether a completed send adds the destination. If it does not, the warning fires forever for any unlabelled address and stops testing "first time" at all; if it does, FR-45's "The user can label addresses locally" is not the only way entries appear. Accepted-context #4 does not cure this one: `docs/decisions.md` §2 has the identical gap ("Any send to an address not already in the local address book shows a … step"), and there is no epic file to point at — §4.11 says so itself.

FR-9's consequence is the most consequential under-specification in the document. "After eight consecutive failures the Wallet requires full re-import from seed" does not say what is destroyed. Is the Vault ciphertext wiped, or is this a lockout with the ciphertext intact and a re-import path as the recovery? For a wallet holding real Mainnet funds those are opposite outcomes for a user who mistypes a PIN eight times and has an imperfect seed backup, and neither is testable from the FR.

### Findings
- **medium** FR-52 contradicts NFR-3 and FR-12 (§4.12 FR-52; §8 NFR-3, NFR-4; §4.2 FR-12; §7 SM-C2) — "Ledger data is network-first and never cached" cannot hold alongside a mandatory query hook with a wallet+network cache key and a lock step that clears "the query cache". *Fix:* restate FR-52's bullet in NFR-4's terms — "no ledger data is persisted to the service-worker cache or to disk; the in-memory query cache is torn down on lock (FR-12)" — and narrow SM-C2 to persisted caching so it stops forbidding the query layer.
- **medium** Address Book membership is undefined, so FR-21 is untestable (§4.4 FR-21; §4.11 FR-45) — the two FRs define each other and neither says whether a completed send creates an entry. `docs/decisions.md` §2 shares the gap and no epic file exists, so the pointer convention does not cover it. *Fix:* state it in FR-45 — either "a completed send offers to add the destination; declining leaves FR-21 firing on the next send" or "entries are created only by explicit labelling, and FR-21 therefore fires on every send to an unlabelled address."
- **medium** FR-9 does not say what a lockout destroys (§4.2 FR-9) — "requires full re-import from seed" is compatible with both a Vault wipe and a recoverable lockout, which are opposite outcomes for a user with a partial seed backup. *Fix:* name the destruction explicitly ("the Vault for that Wallet is erased; recovery is by seed import only") or name the non-destruction, and say whether the count survives a reload.

## 5. Scope honesty — thin

The machinery of scope honesty is all present and mostly load-bearing: §5 does real work (it names the product this is not, and why the two architectural deferrals are architectural), §6.2 names six omissions with reasons, §11 indexes five assumptions, and §9.6 pre-forbids the fabrication this kind of document invites. No item is double-filed between §5 and §6.2 — Web Push, fiat display, telemetry and omnibus addresses sit in §5; Checks/Escrow, multi-signing, RPC endpoints, live-ledger testing, RTL and axe sit in §6.2 — which is cleaner than most PRDs manage. Open-items density (6 questions + 5 assumptions + 2 PM notes for a fifty-six-FR record of built work) is proportionate.

The dimension is *thin* rather than adequate because the PRD cannot hold "permanent" and "deferred" apart, in two independent places, and in the direction that forecloses future work. §5's heading reads "**Permanently deferred for architectural reasons, not absence of effort**" and files both Web Push and fiat-equivalent display under it; FR-28 hardens this to "Background delivery is a permanent non-goal (§5)". But `docs/decisions.md` §2 — which §0 names authoritative for "every decision's reasoning" — says of Web Push "**Deferred** … In-app notification only **for now**", and of fiat "**Explicitly deferred** … no price-oracle dependency **in v1**". `addendum.md` §3 then splits the difference within this same run folder: "Permanently deferred" for Web Push, bare "Deferred" for fiat. The PRD asserts permanence as fact where its own authority says "for now", and §11 carries no assumption tag covering the escalation.

The second instance runs the other way. §2.2 frames multi-signature as a permanent boundary of the product — "Institutional or shared-account holders. One operator, one device, one set of keys; no multi-sig, no omnibus addresses" — while §6.2 files "Multi-signing and regular-key rotation" as merely **Out of Scope for MVP**, i.e. deferred, and even supplies the argument for permanence ("multi-party signing is a different product shape from the single-operator assumption in §2") under an MVP-deferral heading. A reader deciding whether multi-sig is a future epic or a closed door gets opposite answers from §2.2 and §6.2. Note also that omnibus addresses appear in §2.2's list *and* in §5's permanent list, while multi-sig appears in §2.2's list and §6.2's deferred list — the same sentence's two halves are filed under different permanence.

§6.2 also mixes two kinds of thing under one heading. "Checks, Escrow, Payment Channels", multi-signing and user-editable endpoints are product scope decisions. "Automated live-ledger integration testing", and especially "Automated accessibility audit (axe) — unavailable in the build environment", are environment constraints — the axe entry is not a scope choice at all, and it already lives in §9's territory and in `addendum.md` §5's list of manual verification. Filing a tooling limitation as descoped product makes it look decided when it is only blocked. RTL is filed in both registers at once: descoped in §6.2, and open with "no owner and no revisit condition set" in §10 Q4.

### Findings
- **high** The PRD escalates "deferred" to "permanent" against its own authoritative source (§5 heading and bullets; FR-28; cf. `docs/decisions.md` §2 and `addendum.md` §3) — `decisions.md` says Web Push is deferred with "In-app notification only **for now**" and fiat display is deferred "in v1"; §5 files both as permanent and FR-28 calls background delivery "a permanent non-goal". §0 states the record wins where they disagree, so as written the PRD is stale on its own terms, and no §11 assumption tags the upgrade. *Fix:* either restate §5's second block as "Deferred on architectural grounds, not effort — revisiting either requires accepting a server (Web Push) or a third-party price oracle (fiat display)", or, if permanence is the intended product decision, record it in `docs/decisions.md` §2 first and tag it in §11 as an assumption sourced to that edit.
- **high** §2.2 and §6.2 disagree on whether multi-signing is permanent or deferred (§2.2 third bullet; §6.2 second bullet) — "One operator, one device, one set of keys; no multi-sig" reads as a product boundary; "Multi-signing and regular-key rotation — … Out of Scope for MVP" reads as a future epic, and supplies the permanence argument under the deferral heading. The neighbouring "omnibus addresses" from the same §2.2 sentence is filed as permanent in §5, so one sentence's two clauses land in opposite registers. *Fix:* move multi-signing and regular-key rotation into §5 alongside omnibus addresses (the single-operator assumption is the reason for both), leaving §6.2 for things that are genuinely next.
- **medium** §6.2 mixes environment constraints with scope decisions (§6.2, axe and live-ledger testing entries) — "unavailable in the build environment" is a blocker, not a descoping, and it duplicates `addendum.md` §5's manual-verification list. *Fix:* move the axe entry to §9 (Constraints) or leave it to `addendum.md` §5, and reword the live-ledger entry as the standing prerequisite it is rather than as absent scope.
- **low** RTL is filed as both descoped and open (§6.2 fifth bullet; §10 Q4) — harmless individually, but a reader cannot tell whether it is a decision or a question. *Fix:* keep it in §10 only, and have §6.2 point there.

## 6. Downstream usability — thin

The ID layer is sound: FR-1 through FR-56 are contiguous, unique, and every internal cross-reference resolves to the right target. Spot-checked in both directions — FR-5's "(see FR-44)" lands on the truncated-identifier FR; FR-21's Address Book premise lands on FR-45, which points back; FR-41's "(§6.2)" lands on the endpoint deferral; FR-28's "(§5)" lands on the Web Push bullet; §6.2's "the single-operator assumption in §2" lands on §2.2; SM-5's FR-50 and SM-6's FR-44/FR-53–56 are all correct. Sections also extract cleanly: each §4.x names its own Detail pointer, and all fifteen `docs/` paths in the document exist on disk. For a brownfield record feeding epics and architecture, that is the part downstream work leans on hardest and it holds.

The glossary does not hold, and the PRD claims it does — "Terms below are used verbatim throughout this document. Introducing a synonym is a discipline violation." Tested against the document's own text, three things are wrong, and the first is serious enough to be the leading finding of this review.

**Seed is never defined, and Master Key is defined as something XRPL already uses that name for.** §3 defines **Vault** as "the encrypted at-rest store of seed material" and **Master Key** as "one random key generated at setup, wrapped once per configured Unlock Method". "Seed" appears in FR-1, FR-2, FR-3, FR-9 and §11 but has no entry, and nothing in §3 states the relationship between the two — a reader cannot tell whether the Master Key is derived from the seed or independent of it and merely encrypts it. That is precisely the confusion `addendum.md` §2 records as the shipped defect and as "the single most load-bearing piece of reasoning behind FR-8 and NFR-2": the implementation "let the PIN and the passkey each derive their own unverified key rather than unwrapping one shared Master Key". The glossary gap is the documentary form of the bug. Accepted-context #4 does not cover it: §3 points at `docs/user-stories/INDEX.md` § Quick Reference, which is scoped to *XRPL facts every story relies on*, and Seed-versus-Master-Key is an app architecture distinction, not an XRPL fact. No pointer names where it lives.

Compounding it, "Master Key" in XRPL means the account's own master key pair — which is exactly the referent in FR-2's `lsfDisableMasterKey` check, one page below the glossary that has just assigned the term to a local symmetric wrapping key. The PRD therefore uses one capitalised term for two unrelated things, in a document whose §2.2 states that XRPL vocabulary "is used directly, not softened".

### Findings
- **high** Seed is undefined and its relation to the Master Key is unstated (§3; FR-1, FR-2, FR-3, FR-9; cf. `addendum.md` §2) — §3 defines Vault in terms of "seed material" and Master Key as a key "generated at setup", never saying whether one derives from the other. This is the same ambiguity that produced the unlock defect the addendum calls the most load-bearing reasoning in the document, and no pointer covers it (INDEX.md § Quick Reference is scoped to XRPL facts). *Fix:* add a **Seed** entry ("the XRPL secret from which the Wallet's keypair is derived; at rest only inside the Vault") and one clause to **Master Key** stating it is generated independently of any Seed and is the key under which the Vault is encrypted.
- **high** "Master Key" names two different things (§3 Master Key; §4.1 FR-2 `lsfDisableMasterKey`) — the glossary assigns it to the local wrapping key while FR-2 uses the XRPL sense, the account's master key pair, whose disablement is what FR-2 warns about. A downstream story author reading §3 then FR-2 will conflate them. *Fix:* rename the local key (e.g. **Vault Key**, kept as the glossary term wrapped per Unlock Method) and let "master key" keep its XRPL meaning in FR-2, or add an explicit disambiguation line to the §3 entry.
- **medium** The Annunciator has three names (§3 "the docked notice area"; FR-53 "a reserved area"; FR-56 "the reserved area"; §10 Q1 "the empty reserved notice band") — against §3's own rule that "introducing a synonym is a discipline violation", and §10 Q1's "reserved notice band" is the term a reader would have to match to the glossary unaided. *Fix:* use "the Annunciator" in FR-53, FR-56 and §10 Q1.
- **low** Bare "Network" is used as a domain noun but only "Active Network" is defined (§3; FR-41 "per Network", FR-42 "Network-appropriate", §9.2 "the Network's RPC endpoints") — capitalised, so it reads as a glossary term, and is not one. *Fix:* add "**Network** — Mainnet or Testnet as a configuration, independent of which is active" or use "Active Network" / lowercase consistently.
- **low** §9.2's exhaustive claim omits outbound traffic other FRs require (§9.2; FR-40, FR-47) — "The only outbound traffic is to the Network's RPC endpoints and, on user action, to the block explorer" excludes the Testnet Faucet call (FR-40) and the same-origin Release Manifest fetch (FR-47). *Fix:* add both to the list; the Faucet in particular is a third-party service and belongs in a privacy statement that enumerates.

## 7. Shape fit — strong

The shape matches the product on evidence, not by assertion. `PRODUCT.md` records a single-operator product, and the PRD is built as a capability spec: no user journeys (§2.3 argues the absence from the repo's evidence rule rather than leaving a hole), operational rather than user-facing success metrics (§7's correctness gates), and jobs-to-be-done in §2.1 standing in for personas. §0's calibration paragraph pre-empts the obvious objection to its length and gives the right reason — "the stakes are set by the money, not by the audience". Nothing is over-formalised.

As a brownfield document its existing-code references are accurate, which is the load-bearing requirement for this shape and was checked rather than assumed. All fifteen `docs/` paths resolve. `docs/decisions.md` §2, §3 and §4 exist as cited, and §2 contains the quoted "**Yes, add as a new epic**" verbatim. `docs/user-stories/INDEX.md` § Quick Reference exists as cited, lists exactly twelve epic files, and contains no Address Book row anywhere — so §4.11's drift note and §11's fourth assumption are both correct as stated, and the twelve-plus-one feature count in §4's preamble is right. `addendum.md` §7's independent drift claims are of the same quality. A record-of-built PRD earns its credibility here, and this one does.

## Mechanical notes

- **Assumptions Index roundtrips one way only.** All five `[ASSUMPTION]` tags exist exclusively inside §11; there are no inline tags anywhere in §0–§10 (grep: five hits, all in §11's list). Index→body therefore resolves by section reference, but body→index does not exist — a reader at §4.11 or §7 does not see that they are standing on an assumption. §2.3 and §4.11 do argue their assumption in place, which softens it. *Fix:* place the tag inline at each of the five sites and keep §11 as the index.
- **One assumption is missing from the index**: §5's escalation of "deferred" to "permanent" (see the Scope honesty findings) is an inference beyond `docs/decisions.md` §2 and carries no tag.
- **ID continuity is clean.** FR-1..FR-56 with no gaps and no duplicates. SM-1..SM-6 and SM-C1..SM-C3 contiguous. NFR-1..NFR-9 contiguous. No FR, SM or NFR is referenced anywhere in the document that does not exist.
- **Cross-references all resolve.** FR-5→FR-44, FR-21→FR-45, FR-28→§5, FR-41→§6.2, FR-45→FR-21, §6.2→§2, §9.3→FR-46–51 (correct: FR-46–51 are the update-control mechanism; FR-52 is the offline shell), SM-1→FR-16–30 (resolves, but over-broad — see Strategic coherence), SM-2→FR-6–9, SM-5→FR-50, SM-6→FR-44 and FR-53–56.
- **§4 template is applied inconsistently.** §4.1, §4.2, §4.4 and §4.12 carry a **Description:**; §4.3, §4.5–§4.11 and §4.13 do not. **Functional Requirements:** appears in §4.1, §4.2 and §4.4 only. §4.11 has no *Detail:* pointer, correctly and by explanation. Harmless to a human reader, mildly annoying to a source-extracting workflow.
- **Glossary case drift, minor.** §2.2 uses lowercase "drops, reserve, trust line, sequence, destination tag" while §3 capitalises Drops and Trust Line; §2.1's "the true spendable number" is the informal form of **Spendable Balance**. Both read as deliberate prose, unlike the Annunciator and Network cases above.
- **Front matter says `status: draft`** while §6.1 records shipped v1 scope and `updated: 2026-09-12` predates this review. Worth a bump if the document is being adopted as the record.
