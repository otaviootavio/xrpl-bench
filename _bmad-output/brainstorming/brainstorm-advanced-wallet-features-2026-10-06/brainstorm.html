Research printout

What the ledger allows

The facts the session pulled from the XRPL docs, and what was reasoned from them. Every feature above has to fit inside this.

  1. ledger factKey types. secp256k1 (default) and ed25519 only, so a choice at creation is real.
  2. ledger factMaster key is tied to the address forever. A Regular Key (SetRegularKey) can sign almost everything and can be replaced at any time; only one regular key per account.
  3. ledger factThe master key can be disabled (asfDisableMaster) and recovered via the regular key or multisig.
  4. ledger factMulti-signing via SignerListSet: 1–32 signer addresses, weights + quorum, each signer has its own key, fee is (N+1)×, and the signer list costs an owner reserve.
  5. ledger factNo protocol-level parent/child derivation. HD (BIP44 m/44'/144') is a wallet convention, and derived accounts are independent on-ledger.
  6. ledger factPasskeys (WebAuthn) sign with P-256 over a WebAuthn challenge envelope, so they cannot produce an XRPL signature directly. A passkey can gate or unwrap, but cannot be the XRPL signer.
  7. ledger factOne key pair can play three roles: master, regular key, signer-list member. The docs recommend the master offline for emergencies and a regular key for daily use.
  8. ledger factInstitutional topology in the docs: issuing (cold), standby (warm), operational (hot), a small balance refilled from above.
  9. ledger factXaman has a Multisign xApp. Multisig coordination exists elsewhere, so the differentiator is coordinating the whole topology, not multisig alone.
  10. inferenceModel A: one shared account, each phone a signer on its SignerList. A stolen phone is removed via SignerListSet, which needs quorum without the stolen phone.
  11. inferenceModel B: each child phone is its own account, with the master phone's key as its Regular Key or on its SignerList. The master can sweep via Payment, or close via AccountDelete (remaining XRP to a destination).
  12. inferenceAccountDelete has preconditions (no blocking owned objects such as trust lines with a balance, sequence age ~256 ledgers) and burns a fee of one owner reserve.

What it does not allow

  • inferenceNo native spending limits. A child that can sign alone can also drain alone, which means racing the thief, unless the child's own master key is disabled and the child's weight is below quorum.
  • ledger factNo parent/child accounts. "Derived" accounts are strangers to each other on-ledger.
  • ledger factNo passkey signatures. P-256 inside a WebAuthn envelope is not an XRPL signature.
  • ledger factOne regular key. Never two at once per account.

Technique 4 · TRIZ Contradiction

Convenience against safety

Instead of trading one for the other, separate them: in space, by value, by destination, or by handing the safety net to the ledger itself. All by coach.

Convenience
Safety

Separate in space

The test vault and the Mainnet vault are physically separate stores.

Separate by value

Small sends fast, large sends slow.

Separate by destination

Known contacts fast, new addresses slow.

Ledger as safety net

Daily regular key on the phone, master key offline, so phone convenience is recoverable.

Recovery-only signer

A 1-of-2 signer list with the phone plus an offline backup key, purely for recovery.

Narrow the target

Recipient allowlist per wallet.

Separate in time

Build transactions at leisure, sign them all with one auth.

Bundle the auth

Batch transactions (XLS-56 Batch; check Mainnet status) for one auth covering several txs.

Zero extra taps

An undo window gives safety with no added steps.

Say it out loud

Low-security wallets carry a visible "fast lane" badge everywhere.
