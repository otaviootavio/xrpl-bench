### Process lessons

- **P1: A reported high money defect was merged around, not stopped on.** The unattended loop has a way to *report* (the PR body, deferred-work) and a way to *merge*, but no rule that a high-severity money finding blocks the next epic or creates a story at once. Epic 9 (#41) merged after #40 while the cap was known to be missing. The epic 5 precedent (retro F1 "ships before epic 6") shows the right shape: a money defect gets an explicit ordering constraint.
- **P2: "Proposed for epic N" in deferred-work is not a hand-off.** Three entries routed to epic 7 by the 2026-10-04 triage were never put in front of the epic 7 build session as scope or as an explicit decline. The triage wrote a destination, and nobody read it at the destination.
- **P3: A mock that records arguments verifies nothing about the SDK contract.** Epic 2's review treated "the fake saw the option" as "the cap is pinned". A type cast hid the contract mismatch from `tsc`, and the fake hid it from the tests. Only reading the SDK source (epic 7) or a real-network call (this retro's probe, #42's Testnet pass) found it.
- **P4: "No UI change, so no browser pass" was reasonable for 7.1–7.3**, which change no rendered output. But the one real-ledger observation that mattered here (fee shown ≠ fee charged) had already been made in a browser pass, in epic 5. It was the non-browser process that dropped it.
